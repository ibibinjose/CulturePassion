# CulturePass — Outreach Playbook

**Owner: CEO** · Covers sponsors, government and grant bodies, investors, and community partners.

> ## ⚠ NOTHING IN THIS FOLDER HAS BEEN SENT
> Every email in `07-outreach/` and `07-outreach/ready-to-send/` is a **draft prepared for your review**. No message has been sent to any sponsor, funder, investor or partner. Before anything goes out you need to: fill every `[bracketed]` field, verify the recipient and their current role, confirm the claims in §2 below are still accurate, and send it yourself from your own account.

---

## 1. Sequencing — do this in order

The order matters more than the copy. Sending investor emails before the pilot has any evidence, or sponsor emails before you can describe the audience, wastes your best contacts on your weakest pitch.

| Wave | When | Audience | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Now (Aug–Sep 2026) | **Community partners** | Costs nothing, unlocks everything. A peak-body MoU introduces 40 organisations and is the evidence base for every later conversation. |
| **2** | Sep–Oct 2026 | **Councils** (free partnership offer) | No procurement friction. Generates the data that makes a paid civic conversation possible in 6–12 months. |
| **3** | Sep–Nov 2026 | **Grant bodies** | Rounds open on fixed calendars. A missed annual round costs 12 months, so this cannot wait for convenience. |
| **4** | Oct–Dec 2026 | **Sponsors** | Needs the pilot underway so you can describe real supply, even without an audience yet. |
| **5** | Nov 2026–Feb 2027 | **Investors** | Needs 60+ days of pilot data. Every week of evidence materially improves the conversation. |

**The exception:** if an investor introduction appears warm and unprompted, take the meeting immediately. Warm intros do not keep.

## 2. What you may and may not claim — read before every send

| ✅ True as at August 2026 | ❌ Not true — do not imply |
|---|---|
| Platform built and operating: 39 screens, 15 API modules, production AWS infrastructure | Any host, participant, attendance or revenue figure |
| Signed-QR passes; duplicate/cancelled/wrong-event scans rejected at the gate | "Trusted by X organisations" |
| Culture Passion Awards designed, governed, budgeted | Any partnership before an agreement is signed |
| Melbourne pilot launching September 2026 | Any grant, sponsor or council logo before execution |
| Free-RSVP events never attract a fee — enforced architecturally | "Award-winning" |
| Individual-level data never sold — aggregate only, small-cell suppression | Any figure the Data & Impact function cannot evidence |

**The rule:** every forward-looking number is labelled as a target. Say "we are targeting 320 organisations in year one," never "we work with 320 organisations." Founders lose credibility permanently on exactly this, usually in a first meeting, and it is completely avoidable.

## 3. Cadence by audience

### Community partners — relationship-led, no sequence

| Touch | Timing | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | Email or, better, a phone call. Ask to meet at their venue. |
| 2 | Day 7 | In-person meeting. Show the feed on a phone. Publish something with them. |
| 3 | Day 14 | Written offer: free Professional for all members, co-branded page, aggregate reporting |
| 4 | Day 30 | Follow up. Then leave it. |

Never automate this. Never send a sequence. Community organisations recognise a drip campaign instantly and it costs the relationship permanently.

### Councils

| Touch | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | Email to the **events or libraries team, not the strategy team** — they publish constantly and have the least friction |
| 2 | Day 7 | Follow up with one specific: an event in their LGA already on the platform |
| 3 | Day 21 | Offer the free partnership. No cost, no procurement. |
| 4 | Day 45 | Share their own suburb's data, once there is some |
| 5 | Quarterly | Keep warm until their budget cycle turns over |

**Always ask, by the second meeting: who holds the budget, and when does their financial year turn over?** Enthusiasm from someone without budget authority costs six months.

### Grant bodies

| Touch | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8+ weeks before deadline | Pre-application enquiry — confirm fit and eligibility before writing anything |
| 2 | On reply | Specific questions about assessment criteria |
| 3 | ≥3 days before deadline | Submit |
| 4 | Post-outcome | Thank them; request feedback whether successful or not |
| 5 | Ongoing | Maintain the relationship. Most funders fund repeatedly; the second application is always easier. |

### Sponsors

| Touch | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | Personalised email referencing their **actual published** community commitment |
| 2 | Day 7 | Follow up with one concrete data point or a relevant host story |
| 3 | Day 18 | Final touch: the prospectus, and an explicit "should I stop?" |
| 4 | Day 90 | Nurture — a genuine update, not a re-pitch |

Three touches, then stop. A fourth chase converts almost nothing and costs the FY28 conversation.

### Investors

| Touch | Timing | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day 0 | Warm intro strongly preferred. Cold only with a genuinely specific thesis hook. |
| 2 | Day 5 | One-pager + offer of a live demo |
| 3 | Day 14 | Traction update — the single most effective follow-up available |
| 4 | Monthly | Add to the investor update list even if they passed. Passes reverse on traction more often than founders expect. |

## 4. Personalisation — the minimum bar

A template sent unmodified is worse than no email. Before every send, at least:

| Audience | Minimum personalisation |
|---|---|
| Community partner | Name a specific event or program they run |
| Council | Name a specific event in their LGA, or their current cultural strategy by name |
| Grant body | Quote their program objectives verbatim, in their words |
| Sponsor | Cite their **actual published** DEI, CSR or community commitment. If you cannot find it, do not send. |
| Investor | Name a portfolio company and the thesis connection |

**If you cannot personalise it, the prospect is not researched enough to contact.** That is the test.

## 5. Tracking

One tracker, these fields, no exceptions: organisation · contact name · role · **role verified (date)** · audience type · source of introduction · first contact date · touch count · stage · next action · next action date · objection raised · outcome.

| Discipline | Why |
|---|---|
| Verify the contact's current role before sending | Sending to someone who left 18 months ago is the most common and most avoidable own goal in outreach |
| Log every objection verbatim | Three prospects raising the same objection means the pitch is wrong |
| Stop at the stated touch limit | Over-chasing is worse than not contacting |
| Record who introduced you | Warm intros are the scarcest resource; know who provides them and close the loop with them |

---

## 6. Follow-through — where outreach actually fails

Outreach does not usually fail at the first email. It fails after a positive reply, when nothing happens for three weeks.

| Commitment | Standard |
|---|---|
| Reply to any inbound response | Within 24 hours, always |
| Send promised material | Within 48 hours, in the same thread |
| After a meeting | Written summary within 24 hours, with the agreed next step and a date |
| No next step agreed | Then the meeting did not conclude. Ask for one before it ends. |
| If they go quiet | One follow-up, then move on gracefully. Leave the door open. |

## 7. Email hygiene

- Send from a real, monitored address. `cultureos.co@gmail.com` works for now, but a domain address (`bibin@culturepass.app`) is materially more credible for government and corporate recipients — **set this up before Wave 3.**
- Subject lines under 60 characters, specific, no marketing language.
- Under 200 words. Government and corporate recipients read on phones between meetings.
- One ask per email. Two asks get zero.
- No attachments on a first email — link instead, or offer to send.
- Plain text or very light formatting. Designed HTML emails read as bulk mail and are filtered accordingly.
- Signature: name, role, one line on the company, phone, and one link.
- Never BCC a group of prospects. It will be noticed.
- Check the recipient's name spelling. Every time.
