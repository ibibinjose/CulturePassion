# Outreach Emails — Community Partners, Hosts & Universities

**Status: DRAFTS. Nothing sent.**
**These are Wave 1 — send first.** They cost nothing, unlock everything, and produce the evidence base for every other conversation.

> ## The one rule for this audience
> **Never automate any of this.** No sequences, no drip campaigns, no mail merge. Community organisations recognise automated outreach instantly and it costs the relationship permanently. Where possible, phone instead of email, and meet at their venue rather than yours.

## C2 — Individual host (prefer a phone call; use this only if email is the only route)

**Subject:** `[Their event] — free listing?`

> Hi [First name],
>
> I saw [their specific event] — [one genuine, specific observation about it].
>
> I'm launching CulturePass in Melbourne this September. It's a place where events like yours can be found by people who'd love to come but have no way of hearing about them.
>
> It's free to publish. If you ever sell tickets it's 3.5% plus 80 cents, which works out at about A$1.22 on a A$12 ticket — most platforms would take three or four times that. Free events are free, always.
>
> One thing that might be more useful than the listing: at the end of each event you get a report — how many came, where from — that you can put straight into your next funding application.
>
> I'd rather show you than explain it. Could I come by for fifteen minutes and put your next event up with you? Takes about three minutes.
>
> **Bibin Jose** · CulturePass
> [phone]

**Notes.** Name the actual fee in dollars, not just percentages. Vagueness about money reads as a trap to an organisation that has been burned by platforms before.

## C3 — Place of worship / faith community centre

**Subject:** `Free listing for [organisation]'s community events`

> Dear [Title and name],
>
> I'm writing about a free service that may be useful for [organisation]'s community and cultural events.
>
> CulturePass is a platform launching in Melbourne this September where community organisations list events at no cost, and local residents can find them. Search works in community languages, and there is a map and calendar.
>

>
> You control what is listed and what is not. If there are gatherings you would prefer not to be publicly visible, they simply aren't listed. For events where exact location shouldn't be public until someone has registered, that's supported too.
>
> Would you be open to a short conversation, or would it be better if I visited?
>
> With respect,
>
> **Bibin Jose** · Founder, CulturePass
> [phone]

**Notes.** The control-and-privacy paragraph is essential for this audience and it should not be trimmed. Some communities have entirely legitimate safety reasons for restricting venue visibility, and leading with that acknowledgement is the difference between being trusted and being another platform.

## C4 — First Nations organisation (relationship-led — read the caution)

> ## ⚠ Do not send a template to a First Nations organisation
> This is a starting point for a **conversation**, not an email to send. Approach through an existing relationship where one exists. Move on their timeframe, not the pilot's. If it produces two relationships rather than five, that is an honest outcome — and inflating the number with a campaign push would be worse than the shortfall.

**If a written introduction is genuinely the appropriate route:**

> Dear [First name],
>
> I'm writing to introduce myself and to ask whether a conversation would be welcome, at whatever time suits you.
>
> I've built CulturePass, a platform launching in Melbourne where community organisations list cultural events free and residents can find them. I'm conscious that platforms have a poor record with First Nations cultural material, and I'd rather ask than assume.
>
> The commitments built in, which I'd want to talk through properly:
>
> - Organisations control their own profile, and can restrict how data about their activity is used, including in aggregate reporting
> - Cultural knowledge shared on the platform remains the property of the community it belongs to
> - Our Cultural Advisory Council includes First Nations members, and questions of First Nations cultural authority are decided by them, not by me
> - Aggregate reporting follows Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles. Where a community doesn't want its activity reported, it isn't reported.
>
> I'm not asking for anything. If a conversation would be useful, I'd welcome it, and I'm happy to be told the timing isn't right.
>
> **Bibin Jose** · Founder, CulturePass
> [phone]

**Notes.** Repeating their concern back in writing is the highest-value line in this email. It demonstrates you were listening rather than pitching, and with community partners that distinction is the whole relationship.
