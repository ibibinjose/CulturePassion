## A1 — Organisation summary (100 words)

> CulturePass is an Australian technology platform that makes cultural events findable, joinable and measurable. Community organisations publish events free of charge; residents discover them through a mobile-first feed with search in community languages; and organisers receive participation data they can use in their own funding applications. The platform is built and operating, with production infrastructure, digital ticketing and verified check-in at the door. CulturePass launches in Melbourne in September 2026 across six suburbs with high concentrations of multicultural community organisations. Its purpose is to remove the discovery and capability barriers that keep grassroots cultural activity invisible to everyone outside the community running it.

## A2 — Problem statement (250 words)

> Grassroots, diaspora and traditional cultural organisations face structural barriers that are not of their own making.
>
> Their events reach audiences only through closed channels — WhatsApp broadcast lists, community Facebook groups, printed flyers in local shops. Discovery therefore requires prior membership of the right network. The result is a hard ceiling on participation: the same families attend year after year, while a city of millions has no route in. The people most excluded are precisely those cultural policy is designed to reach — recently arrived migrants without an established network, second and third-generation Australians reconnecting with heritage, and residents who would participate across cultures if they knew what was on.
>
> Organisers face a parallel barrier. Ticketing infrastructure is priced for high-value tickets and consumes 15 to 20 per cent of a A$12 community ticket, so organisers take cash at the door. Without ticketing they have no pre-sale income to secure a venue, no attendance record, and therefore no evidence for their next funding application — which entrenches exactly the disadvantage the funding was intended to relieve.
>
> Funders face the third barrier. There is currently no instrument for measuring cultural participation at community granularity. Attendance figures are self-reported, unverifiable, and not comparable between organisations or across years. Questions as basic as "is participation growing, or are we reaching the same people repeatedly?" cannot be answered in any Australian jurisdiction.
>
> These three barriers are interdependent. None can be resolved alone, and together they keep a large part of Australia's cultural life outside the systems that fund, measure and support culture.

## A3 — Proposed activity (200 words, adapt the numbers)

> Over [12 months], CulturePass will deliver a bounded program of community onboarding and digital capability support in [area]:
>
> **1. Onboarding.** Recruit and support [120] cultural organisations, prioritising [named communities and suburbs]. Support is delivered in person, at the organisation's own venue, with staff publishing the organisation's next event alongside them.
>
> **2. Capability.** Provide digital capability materials and support in [nine] languages — [list] — through written guides, short video, telephone support and in-person sessions. Materials are human-translated, not machine-translated.
>
> **3. Publishing.** Publish [300+] cultural events to a free, publicly accessible discovery platform with search in community languages, map and calendar views.
>
> **4. Participation.** Enable [25,000] verified participations, measured at the door through digital check-in rather than estimated afterwards.
>
> **5. Reporting to organisations.** Provide every participating organisation with a participation report they can use directly in their own funding applications, at no cost.
>
> **6. Reporting to [funder].** Deliver an aggregate participation report covering community engagement, cultural representation, geographic dispersal and economic impact, with individual-level data excluded and small-cell suppression applied.
>
> All participating organisations retain free access permanently. Free-RSVP events never attract a platform fee.

## A4 — Community need and consultation (150 words)

> The need was identified through direct engagement with cultural organisers in Melbourne, and the design of the platform reflects what they described: that their events are invisible to anyone outside their existing membership, that available ticketing tools do not work at community price points, and that they cannot evidence attendance for funding purposes.
>
> Consultation continues structurally rather than as a one-off exercise. A paid Cultural Advisory Council of six to nine members — including First Nations representation and rotating diaspora community representation — holds binding authority over cultural taxonomy, verification standards and any representation dispute. Council members are remunerated; unpaid community advisory bodies reproduce the extraction this program exists to counter.
>
> Community partners are engaged as partners rather than beneficiaries: participating peak bodies receive free access for all their member organisations and aggregate participation reporting they can use for their own funding and advocacy.

## A5 — Outcomes and measurement (200 words)

> Outcomes are measured across four families, all collected automatically and continuously by the platform rather than reconstructed at reporting time:
>
> **Community engagement** — verified attendances segmented by cultural community, from digital check-in at the door. Verified check-ins and estimated attendance are always reported as separate figures, with the estimation method stated.
>
> **Cultural representation** — the count of distinct cultural, linguistic and First Nations communities with active organisations and events on the platform, which identifies under-served communities relative to population.
>
> **Economic impact** — gross revenue generated for small-scale and non-profit hosts through platform ticketing, reported directly from transaction data.
>
> **Geographic dispersal** — attendance by suburb, and the share of attendance occurring outside the central business district.
>
> **Organiser capability** — the number of organisations independently publishing events after receiving support, measured by publishing activity rather than by self-report.
>
> Baselines are zero for all measures, as the platform launches in September 2026.
>
> Because measurement is a product feature rather than an evaluation add-on, reporting is available continuously throughout the funding period rather than only at acquittal — and the same data is available to participating organisations for their own use.

## A6 — Organisational capability (200 words)

> **Platform.** CulturePass is built and operating, not proposed. The production platform comprises 39 user-facing screens, 15 backend service modules and 11 database schema groups, deployed on Amazon Web Services infrastructure in the Sydney region using infrastructure-as-code. It includes digital ticketing, signed-token QR passes, and gate check-in that rejects duplicate, cancelled and wrong-event scans.
>
> **Leadership.** Founder and Chief Executive Bibin Jose designed and built the platform end to end, including the front end, backend services, data model and cloud infrastructure.
>
> **Governance.** A board is formed at seed close, including an independent chair. A paid Cultural Advisory Council holds binding authority on cultural matters, including a veto on commercial activations using cultural material.
>
> **Compliance.** A privacy impact assessment is completed before launch. Accessibility is audited to WCAG 2.2 AA. Child Safe Standards compliance applies where events involve minors. All Australian data is held in Australian infrastructure. Individual-level data is never sold or shared.
>
> **Track record.** CulturePass has not previously delivered a funded program. This is stated plainly. The evidence of capability offered instead is a built and operating platform, documented governance, and named community partners.

## A7 — Sustainability beyond the grant (150 words)

> The funded activity does not create ongoing dependence on grant support.
>
> CulturePass operates a commercial model — ticketing commission, organiser subscriptions, brand sponsorship and civic data licensing — which funds continued platform operation and development independently of grant income. Grant funding is sought specifically for the community outreach, in-language capability support and translation work that has no commercial return but is essential to reaching under-served communities. That is the part a commercial model structurally cannot fund, and it is the part being requested.
>
> Organisations onboarded through this program retain free access permanently. Free-RSVP events never attract a platform fee, which is enforced in the platform's architecture rather than as a pricing policy that could later be reversed. Participation data generated during the funded period remains available to each organisation indefinitely for their own funding applications.
>
> The measurement infrastructure built under this grant continues operating and improving after the funding period ends.

## A8 — Value for money (120 words)

> The requested amount of A$[X] delivers [N] organisations onboarded and [N] verified participations, a cost of approximately A$[X] per participation and A$[X] per organisation supported.
>
> Three factors make this efficient. First, the platform already exists — the grant funds community delivery, not software development. Second, onboarding is leveraged through partnerships: a single agreement with a peak body introduces its full membership rather than requiring individual recruitment. Third, measurement is automated as a platform feature, so evaluation costs a fraction of a comparable survey-based approach.
>
> CulturePass contributes A$[X] in cash and in-kind, including staff time, platform operation and infrastructure costs. Administration is capped at 10 per cent of the grant.

## A9 — Risk management (150 words)

> **Insufficient organisation uptake.** Mitigated by concentrating on six suburbs rather than spreading across the city, and by partnership-led recruitment through peak bodies. Progress is reviewed weekly per suburb, and field resources reallocated to under-performing areas.
>
> **Organisations onboarded but not publishing.** Every new organisation receives a check-in at day seven. Where a barrier is caused by the platform, it is treated as a priority development issue rather than a training problem.
>
> **Cultural representation concerns.** The Cultural Advisory Council holds binding authority over representation disputes. The organisation does not adjudicate cultural authority.
>
> **Privacy.** Individual-level data is never shared. All external reporting is aggregated with small-cell suppression and passes privacy review before release.
>
> **Participant safety.** Host attestations at publication, insurance verification for higher-risk categories, Child Safe Standards compliance, and a documented incident response procedure.
>
> **Delivery capacity.** Key-person dependency on the founder is addressed by recruiting a senior engineer as the first hire.

## A10 — First Nations engagement (150 words)

> CulturePass engages with First Nations organisations on a relationship-led basis, on their timeframe, and not as a recruitment target with a deadline.
>
> Three commitments govern this. First, First Nations organisations control their own profiles and can restrict how data about their activity is used, including in aggregate reporting. Second, Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property is respected: cultural knowledge shared on the platform remains the property of the community it belongs to. Third, the Cultural Advisory Council includes at least two First Nations members, and questions of First Nations cultural authority are determined by those members rather than by the organisation.
>
> Events involving First Nations culture require the host to attest to community authority or partnership.
>
> Aggregate reporting that identifies First Nations activity follows Indigenous Data Sovereignty principles. Where a community does not wish its activity reported, it is not reported.

## A11 — Accessibility and inclusion (120 words)

> The platform is designed mobile-first, recognising that many users in target communities access the internet primarily by phone.
>
> Accessibility is audited to WCAG 2.2 AA before public launch. This is treated as a delivery prerequisite rather than an enhancement, and it is also a condition of government procurement.
>
> Materials, the nomination and onboarding flows, and support content are provided in nine languages — English, Mandarin, Arabic, Vietnamese, Hindi, Punjabi, Greek, Italian and Malayalam — with human translation review rather than machine translation alone.
>
> Non-digital pathways are maintained deliberately: telephone support and printed materials at partner venues, so that organisations with low digital capability are not excluded by the very platform intended to include them.
>
> Free events are free to publish and free to attend, permanently.

---

## A12 — Partnerships (100 words)

> CulturePass works through community partners rather than around them. Partner organisations — multicultural peak bodies, ethnic community councils, migrant resource centres, local councils, libraries and arts organisations — receive free Professional-tier access for all their member organisations, a co-branded collection page, and aggregate participation reporting they can use in their own funding and advocacy.
>
> This structure is deliberate. A single partnership agreement can introduce forty member organisations, which is both more efficient than individual recruitment and more trustworthy from the member's perspective, because the introduction comes from an organisation they already rely on.
