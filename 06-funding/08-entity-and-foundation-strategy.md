**August 2026** · Decision memo for the founder and (once formed) the board.
**This is a strategy memo, not legal or tax advice.** Every structural step below needs sign-off from an Australian charity/tax lawyer and an accountant before execution. The cost of getting this wrong is losing DGR endorsement retrospectively, which is materially worse than the cost of an hour of advice.

## 2. The Pty Ltd — not really optional

You need an Australian proprietary company limited by shares before almost anything else in the funding plan can happen:

| Requirement | Why the Pty Ltd is needed |
|---|---|
| Seed equity round | Investors subscribe for shares. There is nothing to invest in without it. |
| R&D Tax Incentive | An "R&D entity" must be a company incorporated under Australian law and **liable to income tax**. A tax-exempt charity is excluded. The most reliable non-dilutive line in the whole funding plan lives here and cannot be moved. |
| Stripe Connect, merchant facilities, holding ticket funds | Contracted in the company's name |
| Employment, insurance, IP ownership | Company holds all of it |
| Civic and sponsorship contracts | Commercial counterparty |
| Founder equity, ESOP for the first five hires | Requires share capital |

### Naming — resolve this before registering

There are currently three names in circulation: **CulturePass** (product), **CultureOS** (entity, per the email address), and **CulturePassion** (in the question above). Three names for one thing is brand dilution, and it also creates a practical problem: a grant assessor or investor who searches for one name and finds another reads it as disorganisation.

Recommended resolution:

| Layer | Name | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Operating company | **CulturePass Pty Ltd** | Matches the product and the domain. Simplest story. |
| Product / brand | **CulturePass** | Already built, already in the app |
| Awards program | **Culture Passion Awards** | Already designed under this name; the "passion" wordplay works for a recognition program in a way it does not for infrastructure |
| Charitable entity (later) | **Culture Passion Foundation Ltd** | Ties the foundation to the awards, which is exactly the charitable activity |

That gives a clean split: *CulturePass* is the infrastructure business, *Culture Passion* is the recognition and grant-making arm. Two names with two clearly different jobs, rather than three names for the same job.

### Pre-registration checks

- ASIC company-name availability, plus business-name registration for any trading names
- **Trade mark searches at IP Australia for "CulturePass" and "Culture Passion Awards"** — do this before spending money on brand. "CulturePass"-style marks exist in several jurisdictions (including European "Kulturpass"/culture-pass government schemes), so international clearance for the FY29 markets is a separate and later check, but the Australian check should happen now.
- Domain and social handle consistency with whichever name wins

## 3. The Foundation — the real argument for it

The case is not philosophical. It is that **a large class of money is legally unreachable without it.**

| Funder type | Can fund a Pty Ltd? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Venture capital / angels | ✅ | Pty Ltd only |
| R&D Tax Incentive | ✅ | Pty Ltd only (must be liable to income tax) |
| Most state and federal arts/multicultural grants | ⚠️ Sometimes | Many accept companies; some require incorporated not-for-profit status or an auspice |
| Local council grants | ⚠️ Sometimes | Frequently restricted to not-for-profits or require auspicing |
| **Private ancillary funds (PAFs) and most philanthropic trusts** | ❌ | Generally can only distribute to **DGR Item 1** entities. This is a hard legal constraint on the funder, not a preference. |
| **Large Australian foundations** (e.g. Ian Potter, Myer, Lord Mayor's Charitable Foundation, Helen Macpherson Smith) | ❌ | DGR1 + ACNC registration effectively required |
| Corporate CSR / workplace giving | ⚠️ | Much of it is structured to require DGR for tax-deductibility |
| Individual donations | ⚠️ | Not tax-deductible without DGR, which materially reduces giving |

**The gap this closes:** the funding strategy targets A$2.55M of grant income across FY27–FY29. A meaningful share of the philanthropic component of that pipeline is closed to a Pty Ltd no matter how good the application is.

### The charitable purpose is genuinely available

Under the Charities Act 2013 (Cth), **"advancing culture"** is a recognised charitable purpose, and so is **promoting reconciliation, mutual respect and tolerance between groups in Australia** — which is an unusually precise description of what this platform does. There is no need to contort the purpose to fit.

### DGR pathway — current position, and it changed recently

1. **ACNC charity registration is now a precondition for DGR endorsement.** Register with the ACNC first.
2. For cultural organisations, DGR is category **item 12.1.1**, and **since 1 January 2024 the ATO administers it directly**. The old Register of Cultural Organisations (ROCO) list, previously run by the Office for the Arts, has been removed and its published list retired — ABN Lookup is now the source of truth for an entity's DGR status.
3. Core requirements: principal purpose is promoting one or more specified cultural forms (music, visual arts, crafts and design, literature, film, performing arts, Indigenous arts and languages, movable cultural heritage), a gift fund with compliant winding-up and revocation clauses, and appropriate responsible persons.

**The "principal purpose" test is where this gets decided.** A foundation whose principal purpose is *making grants to cultural practitioners and recognising cultural preservation work* has a strong case. A foundation whose principal purpose looks like *promoting a commercial software platform* does not — and the ATO will read the substance, not the constitution.

## 4. The argument against doing it now

| Cost | Reality at 6 FTE, pre-launch |
|---|---|
| Governance | Separate entity, own constitution, own board with a majority independent of the Pty Ltd, responsible-person obligations |
| Compliance | ACNC Annual Information Statement; financial reporting; **reviewed accounts once revenue exceeds A$500k, audited above A$3M** |
| Cost | Realistically A$15,000–40,000 a year in legal, accounting, audit and administration, plus board recruitment |
| Founder time | Two boards, two sets of minutes, two reporting cycles. This is the real cost, and it is the scarcest resource in the company. |
| Related-party scrutiny | Every transaction between Foundation and Pty Ltd needs to be arm's-length, documented and disclosed. The ACNC and ATO both look here. |
| **Structural risk** | A charity seen to be subsidising a for-profit platform risks its endorsement. Retrospective revocation is the worst outcome available. |

**Verdict on timing:** setting this up in Q1 FY27, alongside a pilot launch, a seed round and a payments-compliance blocker, would consume founder attention at precisely the moment the pilot needs all of it — in exchange for grant money that is not yet applied for.

## 5. What to do in FY27 instead: auspicing

**Auspicing** (fiscal sponsorship) is the standard Australian solution to exactly this problem. An existing DGR1 charity — a multicultural peak body, a community foundation, an arts service organisation — receives the grant and contracts CulturePass to deliver the funded project.

| Advantage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Access | Opens DGR-restricted grants immediately, with no new entity |
| Cost | Typically a 5–10% administration fee to the auspice — far cheaper than A$15–40k of standing compliance |
| Speed | Weeks, not months |
| Relationship | The auspice is usually a partner you wanted anyway, and the arrangement deepens that relationship |
| Signal | Demonstrates community endorsement, which reads well to the funder |

**Limits, stated honestly:** the auspice controls the funds and carries the acquittal obligation, you do not build your own funding track record, and it does not work for a program you intend to run permanently. It is a bridge, not a destination.

Good FY27 auspice candidates to approach: Victorian ethnic community councils and multicultural peak bodies, a community foundation, or an arts service organisation already holding DGR1 — several of which are already on the FY27 partnership target list for entirely separate reasons.

## 7. The four triggers for forming the Foundation

Do not form it until **all four** are true. Any one alone is not enough:

1. **A named pipeline of at least A$300,000 in DGR-restricted funding** that has been qualified — specific funders, specific programs, confirmed eligibility requirements. Not "philanthropy exists."
2. **The Awards program has run once successfully**, so the Foundation has a real activity to be constituted around rather than a plan.
3. **A board bench of at least three people willing to serve**, independent of the Pty Ltd — most plausibly drawn from the Cultural Advisory Council.
4. **The company can absorb A$15–40k a year and the founder time** without slowing the pilot or the raise.

## 8. The conflict this creates in the Awards program — and it is a real one

This needs resolving, and it affects a document already written.

[`04-culture-passion-awards/01-program-charter.md`](../04-culture-passion-awards/01-program-charter.md) currently states that **nomination requires a CulturePass community profile.** That is the mechanism that makes the awards an acquisition channel.

**If the Foundation runs the Awards, that requirement becomes a problem on two fronts:**

- **Legally/structurally:** a charity whose grant program is gated on registering with an affiliated for-profit platform is conferring a private benefit on that company. That is exactly the pattern that puts DGR endorsement at risk.
- **Reputationally:** it is also the design choice most likely to make the awards feel like a lead-generation form — which the charter itself identifies as the thing that would stop the flywheel.

### Three options

| Option | Awards run by | Acquisition value | Philanthropic funding | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep awards in the Pty Ltd, keep the profile gate** | Company | Highest | Closed to DGR-restricted funders | Works, but caps the program's funding permanently and carries reputational risk |
| **B. Keep awards in the Pty Ltd, drop the profile gate** | Company | Moderate | Corporate sponsorship only | Cleanest near-term. Recommended for FY27. |
| **C. Move awards to the Foundation, no gate** | Foundation | Moderate, indirect | Open | Right end state from FY28 |

**Recommendation: B in FY27, C from FY28.**

Drop the hard profile requirement now, in the inaugural year, regardless of what happens with the Foundation. Anyone can nominate anyone; nominees do not need to be on CulturePass to win.

**What this costs, stated plainly:** it weakens the supply-acquisition mechanic. The charter's FY27 target of 40 hosts acquired via nomination becomes softer, and should be re-forecast to roughly 25–30. Acquisition then comes indirectly — nominees and voters encounter the platform, and most nominees are exactly the organisations that will join once they see the feed — rather than from a gate.

**Why it is still the right call:** an awards program with contested independence is worth less than no awards program, and the recognition mandate in the charter (§7, the disqualifying failure) cannot survive a design where the award is conditional on signing up. Voting can still require an account — that is a normal integrity control against ballot-stuffing, not a gate on the award itself, and it preserves most of the demand-side acquisition.

## 9. The Cultural Infusion precedent — worth studying, and worth approaching

**Cultural Infusion** is a Melbourne cultural enterprise founded in 2002 by Peter Mousaferiadis, delivering intercultural education programs to more than 375,000 students annually, with a SaaS product line (Cultural Infusion Atlas, launched 2019) and a foundation arm. It holds UN Alliance of Civilizations Intercultural Innovation Award recognition among others.

**Why it matters here:** it is the closest available precedent to the structure being contemplated — same city, adjacent sector, and the same commercial-entity-plus-foundation-plus-technology-product shape. Before committing to a structure, it is worth checking the actual filings rather than the marketing: look up both entities on the **ACNC charity register** and **ABN Lookup** to see the real charity subtype, DGR status, revenue scale, board composition and related-party disclosures. That is 20 minutes of work and it is the single most informative research available on this decision.

**Second reason to pay attention: they are a potential partner, not just a precedent.** Their programs reach schools; CulturePass reaches community events. There is little overlap and obvious complementarity — and an established DGR-endorsed foundation in the same sector in the same city is a credible auspice candidate for FY27.

Worth noting as a talent model either way: a structured internship program is a common and effective pipeline for exactly the roles CulturePass needs cheaply in FY27 (community outreach, translation, content, data). Two cautions if you copy it: **unpaid internships are unlawful in Australia** where the person is doing productive work rather than a genuine vocational-placement requirement, and it is a Fair Work compliance area with real exposure. Any internship program should either be a genuine university placement or be paid.
