# CulturePass — International Grant & Funding Target List

**As at August 2026** · Relevant from FY28 (research) and FY29 (application).

> ## ⚠ VERIFY BEFORE USE
> Same caution as the Australian list, with two additions. **First: most international programs require local establishment** — an entity, a local partner, or activity within their jurisdiction. Do not invest application time before that is resolved. **Second: many require not-for-profit or charitable status in that jurisdiction**, which is a materially higher bar than an Australian auspice arrangement. Every amount, deadline and eligibility rule must be confirmed at source. These ranges are for pipeline sizing only.

**Do not apply internationally before FY29 except where noted.** Applying from Australia for a UK or EU program without local presence wastes 30 hours and burns a first impression with a funder you will want later.

## 5. Gulf Cooperation Council (FY29 — Dubai)

The GCC is structurally unusual and worth understanding before dismissing it: state cultural investment is very large, expatriate and diaspora populations are the majority or near-majority of residents, and government bodies actively procure cultural-participation measurement. **The commercial and civic sale is faster there than in most Western markets.**

| Funder / body | Type | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Dubai Culture & Arts Authority** | Government | Grants and, more importantly, direct commissioning and procurement. The procurement route may matter more than the grant route. |
| **Saudi Ministry of Culture / Cultural Development Fund** | Government | Very large cultural investment programs. Requires local entity and partnership. |
| **Abu Dhabi Department of Culture and Tourism** | Government | Similar structure |
| **Qatar Foundation / Qatar Museums** | Foundation | Cultural programming |
| **Sharjah cultural bodies** | Government | Heritage-focused; a good fit for the Heritage Guardian strand |

**Cautions specific to the GCC:** data residency requirements, local-entity requirements, and cultural governance sensitivity. All three need resolution before entry, not after. This is why Auckland is the FY29 first international market and Dubai is second.

## 8. Three rules for international grant-seeking

1. **Do not apply without local presence or a local partner.** The overwhelming majority of these programs require jurisdictional activity. An application from Australia with no local footing is not a long shot; it is a discard, and it costs a first impression with a funder you will want in two years.

2. **Relationships precede applications by 12–18 months.** International funders fund organisations they have heard of. The FY28 work is conversations, not submissions — and that is a real deliverable, not a delay.

3. **The commercial route is often faster than the grant route.** In the GCC particularly, and increasingly in the UK, a government body will *procure* a participation dashboard more readily than it will *grant* to a foreign company. Check whether the buyer exists before assuming the funder does.
