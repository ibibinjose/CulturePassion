## 2. North Star metric

**Verified cultural attendances per month** — an RSVP or ticket that resulted in a confirmed check-in or a confirmed attendance signal.

Why this and not the alternatives:

| Candidate | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Registered users | Vanity. Measures signup, not participation. |
| GMV | Excludes free events, which are the majority of cultural life and the heart of the mission. Optimising GMV would push us to abandon the community segment. |
| Monthly active users | Measures app-opening, not showing up. We would end up optimising a feed for engagement, which is the Facebook Events failure mode. |
| Events published | Supply-only. Ten thousand events nobody attends is failure that looks like success. |
| **Verified attendances** | **Only metric that requires supply, demand, conversion and real-world follow-through to all work simultaneously. Cannot be gamed without creating actual cultural participation.** |

Targets: **8,000/month by end FY27 · 43,000/month by end FY28 · 150,000/month by end FY29.**

The word *verified* is load-bearing. It is what makes the number saleable to a council and honest to ourselves.

## 3. Strategic pillars

### Pillar 1 — Density
*Be the single best answer to "what cultural thing is on near me this week."*

Density is geographic and temporal, not aggregate. 2,400 events spread across Australia is useless; 400 events in six Melbourne suburbs is a product. Every FY27 decision resolves toward concentration.

| Owns | Key metric | FY27 target |
|---|---|---|
| Community & Partnerships | Published events per target suburb per week | ≥8 |
| | Empty search rate (searches returning <5 results) | <15% |
| | Share of city's known cultural events present on platform | ≥40% in target suburbs |

**Decision rule:** when choosing between breadth and depth, choose depth. Always, in FY27.

### Pillar 2 — Organiser gravity
*Make leaving CulturePass cost the organiser something real.*

Acquisition is not the problem; retention is. Gravity comes from three things a host cannot take with them: their follower list, their attendance history, and their acquittal reports.

| Owns | Key metric | FY27 target |
|---|---|---|
| Community & Product | Month-3 host retention | ≥60% |
| | Hosts who have generated ≥1 acquittal report | ≥120 |
| | Median time from signup to first published event | <72 hours |

**Decision rule:** a feature that increases host retention beats a feature that increases host acquisition, at equal cost, every time.

### Pillar 3 — Civic embedment
*Become the instrument institutions report with.*

The smallest revenue line in FY28 and the largest source of long-term enterprise value. A council that reports statutory cultural participation from our data cannot leave without re-baselining a time series.

| Owns | Key metric | FY27 target |
|---|---|---|
| CEO & Data | Signed civic pilots | ≥1 |
| | Check-in rate on paid tickets (data trustworthiness) | ≥85% |
| | Councils in active pipeline | ≥6 |

**Decision rule:** we do not sell a dashboard before the underlying data is trustworthy. Selling a participation product on weak check-in data would destroy the category's only credible dataset.

### Pillar 4 — Cultural legitimacy
*Be the platform communities recommend to each other.*

The only pillar with no revenue line and the one that gates all the others. Legitimacy is slow to build, instant to lose, and unbuyable.

| Owns | Key metric | FY27 target |
|---|---|---|
| CEO & Cultural Advisory Council | Distinct cultural/linguistic communities represented | ≥45 |
| | First Nations partner organisations onboarded | ≥5, relationship-led |
| | Community partner NPS | ≥50 |
| | Upheld cultural-representation complaints | 0 |

**Decision rule:** any growth tactic that a community partner would describe as extractive is not executed, regardless of its projected return.

## 5. Strategic choices — what we are deliberately not doing

Strategy is mostly the list of things you refuse. These are decided, not open:

| Not doing | Why |
|---|---|
| **Charging for free events** | It would double short-term revenue and end the company's legitimacy. The trust position is the asset. |
| **Scraping or auto-importing events without host consent** | Fastest path to feed density and the fastest path to being rejected by the hosts we need. Consent-based only. |
| **Selling individual-level user data** | Ever. To anyone. Aggregate civic reporting only, with small-cell suppression. |
| **Launching in more than one city in FY27** | Cultural discovery is local. One city done properly beats five done thinly, and we do not yet know what "properly" requires. |
| **Building a general-purpose events platform** | The moment we serve corporate conferences we lose the cultural taxonomy that is the whole product. |
| **Competing with WhatsApp for community coordination** | We are the outward-facing surface the WhatsApp group posts to. Asking communities to abandon their trust networks fails. |
| **Native apps before the PWA is proven** | Distribution cost, review cycles, and two codebases before we know the retention loop works. Shells only in FY27. |
| **International expansion before the AU playbook is documented and repeated twice** | Every failed marketplace expansion looks the same in retrospect. |
| **Raising a Series A into weak host retention** | The gate is real. Contraction is a legitimate outcome; raising into a broken loop is not. |
| **Taking sponsorship money that requires influencing organic ranking** | Editorial firewall. Non-negotiable, in every contract. |

## 6. Sequencing logic

Why this order and not another:

```
Density ──> Organiser gravity ──> Civic embedment ──> Global scale
   │              │                     │                  │
   └── needs      └── needs              └── needs          └── needs
       hosts          density and            trustworthy        a repeatable
       publishing     retention data         attendance data    city playbook
```

Each pillar is a **precondition** for the next, not a parallel workstream:

1. **You cannot retain hosts without density**, because the value they receive is audience reach, which requires a feed people use.
2. **You cannot sell civic embedment without organiser gravity**, because a dashboard covering 15% of a city's cultural activity is not a measurement instrument.
3. **You cannot scale globally without a repeatable playbook**, and playbooks are written from execution, not from planning.

The common failure mode in this category is attempting civic sales in year one because the contracts are large and the buyers will take the meeting. It fails: the data is thin, the pilot underdelivers, the reference is lost, and the market closes for three years. Hence Pillar 3 has a A$0 revenue target in FY27 and one signed pilot — deliberately small, deliberately about proof rather than revenue.

## 7. Operating cadence

| Cadence | Forum | Attendees | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily | Async written standup | All | Blockers surfaced within 24h |
| Weekly | Growth review | CEO, COM, Growth, DAT | Density and retention metrics vs target; one decision |
| Weekly | Product & engineering | CEO, ENG, Design | Ship decisions; roadmap trade-offs |
| Fortnightly | Trust & safety review | TSO, CEO | Moderation SLA compliance, incidents, verification backlog |
| Monthly | Business review | Leadership | Full KPI set, risk register, financial actuals vs model |
| Monthly | Pipeline review | CEO, Sponsorship, Civic | Sponsorship and civic pipeline coverage |
| Quarterly | Board | Board + leadership | OKR grading, re-forecast, capital decisions |
| Quarterly | Cultural Advisory Council | Council + CEO | Taxonomy, verification standards, representation escalations, awards governance |
| Annually | Strategy reset | All + Board + Council | Pillars re-examined, this document rewritten |

**OKR grading discipline:** graded 0.0–1.0 at quarter end. A quarter where everything scores 1.0 means the targets were too soft. Target average is 0.7. Objectives are not moved mid-quarter; if the world changes, the objective is failed honestly and a new one is set next quarter.

## 8. How to use this document

When a decision is contested, resolve it in this order:

1. **Does it violate a §5 "not doing"?** → Stop. The decision is already made.
2. **Which pillar does it serve?** → If none, it is not a priority regardless of who asked.
3. **Does it move the North Star?** → If not, it is at best a hygiene task.
4. **Does the pillar's decision rule resolve it?** → Apply it.
5. **Still contested?** → CEO decides and records the reasoning here, so the next similar decision is faster.

A strategy document that does not get cited in arguments is decoration. Cite this one.
