## 1. Campaign positioning

**The insight:** the people who keep cultural life running have almost never been recognised. Arts awards go to funded institutions and professional practitioners. Community awards go to service delivery. The woman who has run the same festival for nineteen years, unpaid, has no category.

**The campaign line:**

> ## Some people have been holding a culture together for thirty years. Nobody has ever given them anything.
>
> **The Culture Passion Awards. Nominations open now.**

**Supporting lines**
- "Nominate someone who has never been thanked."
- "You know who it is. Five minutes. Any language."
- "Cash, not a certificate."

**What we do not say:** nothing about the platform in the primary campaign. The awards campaign is about the recipients. Platform acquisition happens because people arrive, not because we advertised at them.

## 3. Campaign phases

### Phase 1 — Nominations (Dec 2026 – Jan 2027, 8 weeks)

| Week | Push | Channels |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Launch announcement | Media release, partner briefing, in-app, all social |
| 2 | "What is a Heritage Guardian?" — category explainers | Blog, in-language media, community radio |
| 3 | Partner amplification: 40 organisations circulate to members | Partner newsletters, WhatsApp groups |
| 4 | **Mid-point push** + phone nomination line promoted | Community radio, print, in-person at partner venues |
| 5 | Self-nomination encouragement — the least-used and most-needed route | Social, digest |
| 6 | Category-specific push to whichever category is lagging *(expect Heritage Guardian)* | Direct outreach, elders' groups, places of worship |
| 7 | "Two weeks left" | All channels |
| 8 | Final week countdown; extended if under 200 | All channels |

### Phase 2 — Longlist & voting (Feb 2027, 2 weeks)

- Longlist published in full, unranked, with each nominee's story in their own words where possible
- "Read them all" is the framing, not "pick a winner"
- Nominees given a shareable card for their own networks — **this is the main acquisition moment**
- Vote counts not shown live (prevents a mobilisation contest)
- Daily nominee spotlights across social and digest

### Phase 3 — Ceremony & announcement (Jun 2027)

- Recipients notified privately weeks in advance; nothing leaks
- Media embargo pack: recipient profiles, photography, quotes, and the panel's published reasoning
- Ceremony live-covered with permission
- Recipient profile pieces published as their own assets, in their names
- Sponsors acknowledged; recipients are the story

### Phase 4 — Sustain (Jul 2027 – Jun 2028)

- Recipient spotlight on the platform, ongoing
- Case studies used in grant applications and civic conversations
- 12-month follow-up published: what actually changed for each recipient

## 4. Media approach

### The angle that works

**"Melbourne's cultural life runs on unpaid work — meet the people doing it."**

Lead with a named person and a specific practice. Not with the awards, and never with the company. A story about an 82-year-old teaching a dying craft to four students gets covered. An awards program launch does not.

### The angle that does not work

"App launches awards program." Nobody covers this. If a pitch mentions the platform in the first two sentences, it will not be picked up.

### Targets

| Tier | Outlets | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| Community & in-language | Ethnic press, community radio, 3ZZZ, SBS language programs | Recognition for their community's people, in their language |
| Local | Suburban papers, council communications, hyperlocal digital | The local person nobody knew about |
| Metro | Melbourne dailies, ABC Radio Melbourne, The Age | Human interest: unpaid cultural labour |
| Sector | Arts and community sector press, philanthropy newsletters | A new model for recognising grassroots cultural work |
| National | ABC, SBS | FY28, once there is a track record |

### Spokespeople

- **Recipients speak for themselves.** Always the first offer to any journalist.
- Judging panel members on process and criteria.
- CEO on the program and the company only. Per the brand guide, only the CEO speaks to media on the company's behalf.

## 6. Sample copy

### Media release opening — nominations

> **Melbourne, 1 December 2026** — For nineteen years, [Name] has run [Festival] in [Suburb]. Thousands attend. She has never been paid, and she has never been nominated for anything.
>
> The Culture Passion Awards, opening for nominations today, exist for people like her. Four categories recognise the cultural work that holds multicultural Melbourne together — the elders preserving endangered practices, the volunteer organisers running festivals at scale, the young people reinventing tradition, and the communities collaborating across old divides.
>
> Recipients receive unrestricted grants of up to A$5,000, professional documentation of their work, and recognition from a panel of First Nations and diaspora cultural leaders. Anyone can nominate anyone. Nominations take five minutes and are accepted in nine languages, by phone, and on paper.

## 7. Measurement

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Nominations | ≥400 |
| Nominations in languages other than English | ≥20% |
| Nominations via phone or paper | ≥30 |
| Distinct cultural communities among nominees | ≥25 |
| Public votes | ≥15,000 |
| Participants acquired via voting | ≥5,000 |
| Hosts acquired via awards *(re-forecast, nomination gate removed)* | ≥25 |
| Earned media placements | ≥8 |
| In-language media placements | ≥5 |
| Cost per acquired participant | ≤A$18 |
| **Recipients who would recommend being nominated** | **≥90%** |

The last one governs the others. If the campaign delivers every acquisition target and recipients report it was not worth their time, the program has failed on its own charter and is redesigned before the next cycle.
