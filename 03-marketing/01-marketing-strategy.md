# CulturePass — Marketing Strategy

**Version 1.0 · August 2026** · Owner: Growth & Content Marketer, accountable to CEO
Budget: **A$180,000 FY27 · A$760,000 FY28 · A$1,900,000 FY29**

---

## 1. The strategic constraint that shapes everything

The financial model assumes **participant CAC of A$3.60–4.20**. That number is only achievable if acquisition is community-led. If we end up buying participants through paid channels, CAC moves toward A$18–25 and the marketing line roughly triples, which breaks the model.

So the marketing strategy is not "how do we reach people." It is **"how do we make hosts and community partners do our distribution for us, at a quality we could not buy."**

Everything below follows from that. Paid media is capped at 25% of spend and is used for retargeting and specific event campaigns — never for cold audience building.

## 2. Positioning

### 2.1 Statement

> **For** people in multicultural cities who want to participate in cultural life,
> **CulturePass is** the living calendar of what is actually on — from Malayalam theatre to a Wurundjeri-led walk to a Collingwood warehouse show,
> **unlike** generic event platforms that bury culture in an "Arts & Culture" category or social feeds you have to already be inside,
> **because** we are organised around cultural communities, built with them, and free for free events forever.

### 2.2 Positioning against each competitor, in the words we actually use

| Competitor | Our line |
|---|---|
| Eventbrite | "Built for a A$12 community event, not a A$120 conference ticket." |
| Facebook Events | "You shouldn't have to already be in the group to find out." |
| Council what's-on pages | "Every council's events, in one place, on your phone." |
| WhatsApp groups | "Keep your group. This is how everyone else finds you." |

The last one matters most. **We are additive to community trust networks, never a replacement.** Any messaging that implies communities should abandon their existing channels will be rejected by exactly the hosts we need.

### 2.3 What we never say

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Uber for events", "Airbnb for culture" | Extractive-platform association in a category where that association is fatal |
| "Discover exotic cultures" | Others-people's-culture-as-consumption framing |
| "Authentic experiences" (as a marketing claim about ourselves) | Authenticity is the community's to claim, not our adjective to sell |
| "Disrupting the events industry" | We are trying to be trusted infrastructure, not a disruptor |
| "Unlock hidden gems" | Positions communities as undiscovered resources awaiting extraction |
| Any cultural imagery without a partnering community host | Contractual prohibition, and correct |

## 3. Messaging architecture

### 3.1 Core narrative (one paragraph, used everywhere)

> Every week in Melbourne there are hundreds of cultural events — festivals, performances, food nights, language classes, exhibitions, ceremonies — and most of them are invisible unless you already know someone. CulturePass makes them findable. Communities publish for free, people find events near them in their own language, and organisers finally get the tools and the data they have never had. It is the cultural life of the city, in one place, built with the communities in it.

### 3.2 Audience-specific messaging

| Audience | Their question | Our message | Proof we offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Participants** | "What's on that I'd actually love?" | Your city's cultural life, in one place, in your language | The feed itself — density is the proof |
| **Heritage-connected** | "How do I find my community's events?" | Follow your community. Never miss it again. | Community profiles, language search |
| **New arrivals & students** | "How do I belong here?" | Find your people in your first week | Free events surfaced first, Circles |
| **Hosts (community)** | "Will more people come?" | Reach people beyond your mailing list. Free, always. | Attendance data from comparable hosts |
| **Hosts (festivals)** | "Can I sell tickets without losing 18%?" | 3.5% + 80c. Built for a A$12 ticket. | Fee comparison, plain arithmetic |
| **Hosts (all)** | "Can I prove this for my next grant?" | One-click acquittal reports funders accept | The report itself, shown in the demo |
| **Councils** | "How many people actually participated?" | Verified participation, by community and suburb | Dashboard demo on real data |
| **Sponsors** | "Can I reach diverse audiences credibly?" | Measured reach into communities you cannot buy on Meta | Audience composition from verified data |

### 3.3 Message hierarchy by funnel stage

| Stage | Participant | Host |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | "There's more on than you think" | "Your audience is bigger than your mailing list" |
| Consideration | "Free events near you, this weekend" | "Free to publish. 3.5% + 80c if you sell." |
| Conversion | "RSVP in one tap" | "Publish your first event in three minutes" |
| Retention | "Three events you'll like this week" | "Your post-event report is ready" |
| Advocacy | "Share it with your Circle" | "Your data won you a grant. Tell another organiser." |

## 4. Brand and creative principles

The product's design system is minimal editorial — neutral backgrounds, colour entering only through photography and event content. Marketing follows the same rule, for a specific reason: **the culture is the colour. Our job is the frame, not the picture.**

| Principle | In practice |
|---|---|
| Communities are the heroes | Every campaign features real hosts and real events, named and credited, never stock |
| Photography carries the colour | Neutral typographic frame, vivid cultural imagery inside it |
| Specific beats generic | "Onam sadya in Footscray, Sunday 11am" outperforms "discover cultural events" every time |
| Plain language | Read by people whose first language is not English. Short sentences. No idiom. |
| Never speak for a community | Quote them. Credit them. Pay them if their image sells our product. |
| Multilingual by default | Priority languages: Mandarin, Arabic, Vietnamese, Hindi, Punjabi, Greek, Italian, Malayalam |

**Image rights policy:** any photograph of a cultural event used in our marketing requires host permission in writing. Where an image is used in paid advertising, the host is compensated. This costs money and slows campaigns down, and it is the difference between being a partner and being a scraper.

### 5.1 Host-led distribution — the most important channel

A host who publishes an event has a list, a WhatsApp group, and a following. The single highest-leverage marketing investment we can make is **making it effortless for a host to bring their own audience** — and then having those people stay for everyone else's events.

Mechanics:
- One-tap share to WhatsApp, Instagram Stories, Facebook, and a printable QR poster for physical noticeboards (grocery shops and community halls still matter enormously in this category)
- Auto-generated share images per event, sized for each platform
- Host referral credit: bring another host, both get 3 months of Professional
- Host dashboard shows exactly how many attendees came from their own share vs from CulturePass discovery — this number is what proves our value to them, and it must be honest even when it is unflattering

**Target: ≥40% of new participants arrive via host-led channels.** Tracked weekly. Below 40% is a product gap, not a marketing gap, and escalates as such.

### 5.2 Community partnerships

One MoU with a peak body can introduce 40 member organisations. Priority partner types: multicultural peak bodies and ethnic community councils, migrant resource centres, councils and libraries, places of worship networks, arts peak bodies, First Nations organisations (relationship-led, never campaign-led), universities and student unions, language schools.

The offer to a partner: free Professional tier for all members, co-branded city collection page, aggregate participation reporting for their own funding needs, and a named contact. We are giving them something they need and cannot otherwise get.

### 5.3 Ethnic and community media

Consistently under-priced and over-trusted. Community radio, in-language newspapers, community Facebook pages and WhatsApp channels with genuine reach. Melbourne priorities: SBS radio language programs, 3ZZZ, in-language print, and established community digital publishers.

The approach is editorial, not advertising: a story about local organisers getting tools they never had, with real names. Advertising in community media reads as advertising; a story about the community reads as recognition.

### 5.4 Content & SEO — slow, and the one that compounds

Programmatic pages built from real platform data: `/melbourne/whats-on-this-weekend`, `/melbourne/malayalam-events`, `/footscray/cultural-events`, per-community and per-suburb pages, festival guides (Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid, Onam, NAIDOC Week).

Starts in month 1 for a FY28 payoff, because search intent for "what's on in [suburb] this weekend" is permanent, high-intent, and currently served badly by everyone. By FY29 this should be roughly 25% of acquisition at near-zero marginal cost. **Requires public event pages to be server-rendered** — a Q3 FY27 engineering dependency that marketing must protect on the roadmap.

### 5.5 Paid social & search — deliberately constrained

Capped at 25% of marketing spend. Used only for:
- Retargeting people who viewed an event and did not RSVP
- Specific high-capacity event campaigns, usually co-funded with the host
- Festival-season bursts around known peaks
- Never for cold "discover cultural events" prospecting, which is expensive and converts badly

Exceeding the cap requires CEO approval, because it signals the community-led thesis is failing (risk R12) and that is a strategy problem, not a budget request.

## 6. Funnel and targets

```
Reach ──> Visit ──> Register ──> Activate ──> Attend ──> Return ──> Advocate
```

| Stage | Definition | FY27 target | Primary lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visit | Session on platform | 380,000 | Host shares, SEO, media |
| Register | Account created | 42,000 | Guest browse then contextual signup |
| Activate | Followed a community + RSVP'd/ticketed within 14 days | 45% of registrations | Feed density, onboarding |
| Attend | Verified attendance | 96,000 | Reminders, calendar integration |
| Return | Second attendance within 90 days | 35% | Weekly digest, Passport streaks |
| Advocate | Shared an event or invited to a Circle | 15% | Circles, share mechanics |

**The metric that matters most is Attend, not Register.** Registrations are a vanity number in a discovery product; the North Star is verified attendances (see [`../02-strategy-framework/01-strategic-framework.md`](../02-strategy-framework/01-strategic-framework.md) §2).

## 7. Retention marketing

| Mechanism | Cadence | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly personalised digest | Thursday 17:00 local | The single most important retention asset. Thursday because cultural events cluster Fri–Sun and decisions get made Thursday night. |
| Event reminders | T-24h, T-2h | Reduce no-shows; protect check-in rate, which protects the civic product |
| Followed-community alerts | On publish | High relevance, low volume, highest open rates we will see |
| Cultural Passport streaks & stamps | Continuous | Gamified return visits; feeds awards eligibility |
| Circles invitations | User-initiated | Attending with people is the strongest retention mechanic available |
| Seasonal campaigns | Per festival calendar | Diwali, Lunar New Year, Eid, Onam, NAIDOC Week, Pride, Christmas |
| Host post-event report | T+24h | Host retention, not participant — the highest-value email we send |

**Notification discipline:** every message type is individually controllable, volume is capped, and unsubscribe is one tap. Over-notifying in a community context does not just lose a user — it gets discussed in the community.

## 10. What would make us change this strategy

Stated in advance so the decision is not made emotionally under pressure:

| Signal | Interpretation | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Host-led share below 30% for two months | Host share tooling is inadequate | Escalate to product as P0; do not compensate with paid spend |
| Activation below 30% | The feed is too thin, not the funnel too leaky | Reallocate everything to supply (P01) |
| Paid CAC below A$8 | Unexpectedly efficient; the cap is costing us growth | Bring the cap increase to the CEO with evidence — do not quietly exceed it |
| Organic search growing faster than modelled | The compounding channel is working early | Increase content investment; it is the cheapest long-term acquisition we have |
| A community partner publicly criticises our marketing | We got the framing wrong | Stop the campaign, ask them what to change, credit them for the correction |
