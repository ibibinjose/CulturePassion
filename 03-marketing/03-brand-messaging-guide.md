## 2. Voice

### The four rules

**1. Be specific.** "Onam sadya in Footscray, Sunday 11am" beats "discover cultural events" every single time. Specificity is our whole differentiator, so it has to show up in the copy, not just the product.

**2. Be plain.** Much of our audience reads English as a second or third language. Short sentences. Common words. No idiom, no metaphor, no wordplay. "Find events near you" not "unlock your city's hidden rhythms."

**3. Let communities speak.** We frame; they speak. Quote real hosts. Name them. Credit them. Where their image sells our product, pay them.

**4. Never oversell.** This audience has been marketed at by organisations that wanted something from them. Understatement earns trust. Say what the thing does.

### Voice, calibrated

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Warm | Sentimental |
| Specific | Vague |
| Confident | Boastful |
| Plain | Simplistic |
| Respectful | Deferential to the point of vagueness |
| Direct about money | Coy about fees |

### Before and after

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Unlock authentic cultural experiences in your city" | "See what's on in your neighbourhood this weekend" |
| "Empowering community organisers with cutting-edge tools" | "Publish your event in three minutes. Free." |
| "Join a vibrant community of culture lovers" | "Follow the communities you care about" |
| "Revolutionising cultural discovery" | "Melbourne's cultural events, in one place" |
| "Monetise your events with our platform" | "Sell tickets for 3.5% + 80c. Free events stay free." |
| "Data-driven insights for cultural organisations" | "A report you can put in your next grant application" |
| "Discover hidden gems and exotic traditions" | "Find events from 45 cultural communities across Melbourne" |

## 3. Words

### Use

event · community · host · organiser · participate · attend · neighbourhood · suburb · cultural community · heritage · in your language · free · verified · report · calendar · find · follow

### Avoid, and why

| Word / phrase | Why |
|---|---|
| "exotic", "hidden gems", "undiscovered" | Frames communities as resources awaiting discovery by outsiders |
| "authentic" *(as our claim)* | Authenticity is the community's to claim. We can say "community-hosted"; we cannot certify authenticity. |
| "ethnic events" | Othering. Say "cultural events" or name the specific community. |
| "diverse audiences" *(to participants)* | Fine in a sponsor deck; alienating in consumer copy, where it turns people into a demographic. |
| "Uber for / Airbnb for" | Extractive-platform association, fatal in this category |
| "disrupt", "revolutionise" | We want to be trusted infrastructure, not a disruptor |
| "users" *(externally)* | Say people, participants, hosts. "Users" internally is fine. |
| "monetise" | Never in host-facing copy |
| "leverage", "seamless", "frictionless", "curated experiences" | Corporate filler that signals we are not from this world |
| "multicultural community" *(singular)* | There is no such thing. There are many communities. |

### Terminology, fixed

| Concept | Correct term | Not |
|---|---|---|
| A person attending | participant | user, consumer, attendee (except in host tooling) |
| An organisation publishing | host / organiser | vendor, seller, supplier |
| A group profile | community | group, page, channel |
| Private friend group | Circle | group, squad |
| Digital ticket | pass | e-ticket, QR |
| The loyalty programme | Cultural Passport | rewards, points |
| The awards | Culture Passion Awards | Culture Passion Award *(singular, except a specific one)* |
| Suburb-level area | neighbourhood / suburb | locality, region |

## 4. Visual identity

The product's design system is minimal editorial — neutral backgrounds, colour entering only through photography and event content. Marketing follows exactly the same rule, and for the same reason: **the culture is the colour. We are the frame, not the picture.**

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| Layout | Generous white space, editorial grid, `content-width` container, mobile-first at 390px |
| Typography | `display-font` for headlines, `mono-font` for data and labels; modern sans-serif; large sizes, tight leading |
| Colour | Neutral white/gray/card backgrounds. One accent colour, used only on interactive elements. |
| Imagery | Real events, real people, credited. Vivid inside a neutral frame. |
| Corners | Rounded 20–28px, consistent with the product |
| Motion | Subtle rise-in and fade. Nothing that draws attention to itself. |
| Iconography | Lucide, consistent weight |

### Photography standards

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Real events only | No stock photography of "diverse people laughing". It is visible instantly and it insults the audience. |
| Written permission | Required from the host for any marketing use |
| Compensation | Paid where the image is used in paid advertising |
| Credit | Host and event named wherever the image appears |
| No cultural imagery without a partnering host | Contractual, and enforced by CAC review for sponsor activations |
| Represent the actual mix | Audit imagery quarterly for community representation. If our marketing shows six communities and the platform serves forty-five, we are misrepresenting ourselves. |

## 5. Message library

### Headlines by context

**Consumer acquisition**
- What's on in [suburb] this weekend
- Your city has more culture than you can see
- Find your community's events. Free.
- 45 cultural communities. One calendar.
- New to Melbourne? Start here.

**Host acquisition**
- Your audience is bigger than your mailing list
- Publish your event in three minutes. Free, always.
- Built for a A$12 ticket, not a A$120 one
- The report your next grant application needs
- Free events are free. That is not an introductory offer.

**Institutional / civic**
- Cultural participation you can actually measure
- Who participated, from which communities, in which suburbs
- The evidence your reporting has been missing

**Sponsor**
- Measured reach into communities you cannot buy on Meta
- Support cultural life, and be able to prove it

### Proof points (use these; they are true)

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Built and working | 39 screens, 15 API modules, production AWS infrastructure, all builds clean |
| Free means free | Free-RSVP events cannot accrue a fee — it is architectural, not a pricing promise |
| Fees suit low-price tickets | 3.5% + A$0.80. On a A$12 ticket that is A$1.22. |
| Attendance is verified | Signed QR passes; duplicate, wrong-event and cancelled tickets rejected at the gate |
| Communities have real authority | Paid Cultural Advisory Council with binding authority on cultural matters |
| Data is not sold | Individual-level data never sold or shared with sponsors. Aggregate only, with small-cell suppression. |

### Claims we may not make until they are true

Currently untrue, and using them would be a lie with a timestamp on it:

- Any host, participant, attendance or GMV figure — **there are none yet**
- "Trusted by X organisations"
- "Partnered with [council]" before a signed agreement
- "Award-winning"
- Any grant or sponsor logo before the agreement is executed

**Rule: no number in external marketing that DAT cannot evidence from platform data.** Founders lose credibility permanently on exactly this, usually in a first investor meeting.

## 6. Multilingual standards

| Priority language | Contexts |
|---|---|
| Mandarin (Simplified) | Springvale, Clayton, Box Hill |
| Arabic | Dandenong, Broadmeadows, Coburg |
| Vietnamese | Footscray, Springvale, Sunshine |
| Hindi | citywide |
| Punjabi | Craigieburn, Tarneit |
| Greek | Oakleigh, Brunswick, Preston |
| Italian | Brunswick, Carlton, Preston |
| Malayalam | citywide (and the product's demo host community) |

**Rules**
1. Onboarding materials, host kits and suburb launch materials are translated. Not machine-translated — reviewed by a speaker.
2. Never translate a slogan literally. Rewrite it for the language.
3. Community media placements are written in-language from the start, not translated from English.
4. In-language does not mean simplified. Do not talk down.
5. Host-supplied event content in any language is published as the host wrote it. We do not "correct" a community's description of its own event.

## 7. Crisis and sensitive communications

### Principles

1. **Speed over polish** on safety issues. Say what we know, what we do not, and when we will next update.
2. **Never defend the company at a community's expense.** If a community says we got it wrong, the response starts with listening, not context.
3. **Cultural disputes are not ours to adjudicate.** Route to the Cultural Advisory Council and say so publicly and neutrally.
4. **No apology without a change.** "We're sorry you feel that way" is worse than silence.

### Holding statements

**Safety incident at a listed event**
> We are aware of an incident at [event]. Our immediate concern is the people affected. We are in contact with the organiser and, where relevant, authorities. We will share what we can once we have accurate information.

**Cultural representation dispute**
> A question has been raised about the representation of [community] on CulturePass. We do not make these judgements ourselves. It has been referred to our Cultural Advisory Council, which includes members of the communities concerned, and the listing is paused while they consider it. We will publish their determination.

**Data or privacy concern**
> We have identified [issue]. Here is what happened, who is affected, and what we have done. We have never sold individual-level data and we never will. If you are affected, we have contacted you directly.

**Criticism of commercial model**
> Free events are free on CulturePass and always will be — that is built into how the platform works, not a promotional offer. We charge 3.5% plus 80 cents when a ticket is sold, which is why a A$12 community ticket works here. If you think we have the balance wrong, tell us; our Cultural Advisory Council exists partly for that.

### Escalation

| Severity | Approver | Response time |
|---|---|---|
| Safety incident | CEO | Within 2 hours |
| Cultural dispute | CEO + CAC chair | Within 24 hours |
| Data or privacy | CEO + external counsel | Within 4 hours |
| Product outage | ENG + CEO | Within 1 hour |
| Media enquiry | CEO only | Within 4 hours |

**Only the CEO speaks to media.** Not a hierarchy preference — a single-voice requirement in a category where a careless sentence about a community becomes the story.
