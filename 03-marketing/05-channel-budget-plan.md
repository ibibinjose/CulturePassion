## 3. CAC reconciliation against the model

The financial model assumes participant CAC of A$4.20 (FY27) and host CAC of A$210 (FY27). Reconciling:

- Total GTM spend FY27: **A$275,000**
- Participants acquired: **~42,000** → A$6.55 per participant if all spend is allocated to participants
- Hosts acquired: **~320** → A$859 per host if all spend is allocated to hosts

Neither is right, because spend is joint. The model's split allocates roughly 32% of GTM spend to host acquisition (A$88,000 ÷ 320 = **A$275/host**) and 68% to participant acquisition (A$187,000 ÷ 42,000 = **A$4.45/participant**).

**Both are modestly worse than the model's A$210 and A$4.20.** That gap is real and is worth stating rather than reconciling away: it means FY27 either delivers slightly more volume than planned for the same spend, or the CAC assumptions tighten by roughly 10–30% once host-led distribution matures. The honest position is that **host CAC is the softer of the two assumptions** and the one to watch first. It is a named review item at the month-6 budget review.

## 5. Cost per city (FY28 city launch)

Derived from the Melbourne pilot's A$62,000 for 90 days, adjusted for the playbook existing:

| Line | Per city |
|---|---|
| Field work, travel, materials, translation | 12,000 |
| Suburb kits & printed collateral | 8,000 |
| Ethnic & community media | 20,000 |
| University & student | 8,000 |
| Content & local landing pages | 6,000 |
| Paid retargeting (capped) | 14,000 |
| Host incentives | 6,000 |
| **Total per city launch** | **74,000** |

Higher than Melbourne's pilot spend despite the playbook existing, because Melbourne had founder time at zero marginal cost and warm community relationships that new cities will not. **A launch playbook reduces time and risk, not cash.** Budgeting a city launch cheaper than the pilot because "we know how now" is a common and expensive error.

## 6. Controls

| Control | Threshold | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Paid share of marketing spend | ≤25% | Exceeding requires CEO approval and a board note — it signals the community-led thesis is failing (risk R12) |
| Host-led share of new participants | ≥40% | Below 30% for 2 months escalates to product as P0 |
| Blended participant CAC | ≤A$4.20 FY27 | 20% overrun pauses the worst-performing channel |
| Host CAC | ≤A$210 FY27 | Reviewed at month 6; the softer of the two assumptions |
| LTV:CAC | ≥3:1 | Two consecutive quarters below freezes growth spend entirely |
| Channel attribution | 100% of spend attributable | Unattributable spend is not approved |
| Any single channel | ≤25% of budget | Prevents single-channel dependency |

## 7. Monthly review format

Every month, four questions, in writing, with numbers:

1. **What did each channel cost per participant and per host, actual?** Against plan.
2. **Is host-led share above 40%?** If not, why, and is it product or execution?
3. **Is paid spend under the cap?** If we are near it, what is failing upstream?
4. **What are we stopping?** A channel that has underperformed for two consecutive months gets cut, not optimised. Reallocation beats rescue.

**The discipline that matters:** a channel plan is a hypothesis. By month 6 at least two of the eight channels above will be materially better or worse than modelled. The plan's value is that it makes that discoverable within weeks instead of quarters.
