## Slide 2 — The problem

> ### Cultural participation is large, real, and invisible to infrastructure
>
> **Participants** — discovery requires already being inside the community. WhatsApp lists, closed Facebook groups, flyers in grocery shops.
>
> **Organisers** — mainstream ticketing fees are calibrated for A$80 tickets and consume 15–20% of a A$12 one. So it is cash at the door, a spreadsheet, and no data.
>
> **Funders** — councils and multicultural bodies distribute significant public money and get back attendance estimates written on the night. There is no instrument.
>
> *Three problems, one platform. None is solvable alone.*

**Speaker notes.** The third one is the least obvious and the most valuable — it is the wedge into recurring, sticky institutional revenue that nobody else is positioned to serve. Spend your time there, not on the consumer pain that investors already intuit.

---

## Slide 3 — The solution

> ### Built. Working. Not a mockup.
>
> **Discover** — city feed, natural-language search ("free Malayalam events this weekend"), map, cultural calendar, organised by community not by generic category
>
> **Join** — free RSVP and low-fee ticketing, digital passes with signed QR codes, gate check-in that blocks duplicate, cancelled and wrong-event scans
>
> **Organise** — event wizard, guest lists, co-hosts, verified badges, and grant acquittal reports
>
> **Measure** — first-party analytics producing participation, representation and dispersal data that nobody currently supplies
>
> `39 screens · 15 API modules · production AWS infrastructure · all builds clean`

**Speaker notes.** Offer the live demo here and hold it for the end of the meeting. Building this alone is the strongest available signal about the founder, and it is better shown than claimed.

## Slide 5 — Product & the wedge

> ### Discovery is the wedge. Ticketing is a feature.
>
> The first thing that must be true: CulturePass is the best answer to *"what cultural thing is on near me this weekend."* Everything else is downstream.
>
> **So FY27 supply acquisition is not gated on willingness to pay.** We onboard hosts publishing free events aggressively, because feed density *is* the product.
>
> **Free RSVP events are free forever** — architecturally, not as a pricing promise. We monetise only where money already changes hands.
>
> Then: retention through the grant acquittal report. Then: the civic measurement product.

**Speaker notes.** The obvious challenge is "why not monetise the free events?" Answer directly: it would double short-term revenue and end the company's legitimacy in a category where trust is the only durable moat. Free events are the majority of cultural life and the reason hosts arrive at all.

## Slide 6 — Business model ⭐

> ### Five lines, sequenced so none is load-bearing
>
> | Line | Mechanism | FY27 | FY29 |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Ticketing | 3.5% + A$0.80/ticket | 48k | 1.22M |
> | Organiser SaaS | A$29 / 59 / 99 per month | 18k | 0.70M |
> | Sponsorship | Category-exclusive native placement | 120k | 2.10M |
> | Civic licences | Council participation dashboards | 0 | 0.66M |
> | Culture Passion Awards | Program sponsorship | 0 | 0.90M |
> | **Platform revenue** | | **A$186k** | **A$5.59M** |
>
> Gross margin **66% → 86%** — driven by mix shift toward sponsorship and licences, not by efficiency gains.

**Speaker notes.** Two things to volunteer before you are asked. First, the margin expansion is mix, not leverage — saying so builds more credibility than the number itself. Second, **sponsorship at 38% of FY29 revenue is the largest concentration risk in the model, and a 50% sponsorship shortfall costs nearly twice what a total collapse in paid-ticket share costs.** Naming your own biggest vulnerability is the single most effective credibility move available in a seed meeting.

## Slide 8 — Public-private alignment

> ### Non-dilutive capital as a CAC subsidy, not charity
>
> **A$2.55M grant target across FY27–FY29** — arts and cultural development, multicultural and social cohesion, tourism and visitor economy, digital inclusion, and the R&D Tax Incentive.
>
> **Why this matters commercially:** grant capital pays for market-entry CAC in communities where commercial CAC would never justify the spend. Those are exactly the communities that constitute the community graph — the moat.
>
> **Grants are additive, not load-bearing.** The business is EBITDA-negative with or without them, and this round is sized on the commercial case alone.

**Speaker notes.** Investors are usually sceptical of grant-dependent plans, and rightly. Pre-empt it: grants are not in the base case as a survival mechanism, they compress the CAC of the hardest-to-reach and most strategically valuable supply. Mention that a foundation structure in FY28 unlocks philanthropic capital that is legally closed to a Pty Ltd.

## Slide 9 — Financials & unit economics ⭐

> | | FY27 | FY28 | FY29 |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Cities | 1 | 5 | 8 |
> | Active hosts | 320 | 1,600 | 4,800 |
> | Attendances | 96k | 520k | 1.8M |
> | GMV | A$0.80M | A$5.30M | A$21.4M |
> | Platform revenue | A$186k | A$1.53M | A$5.59M |
> | Gross margin | 66% | 85% | 86% |
> | EBITDA excl. grants | (1.00M) | (2.37M) | (3.97M) |
> | Headcount | 6 | 17 | 38 |
>
> **Host CAC A$180 · 3-yr host LTV A$728 · LTV:CAC 4.0:1 · Participant CAC A$3.60**
>
> LTV uses **host-attributable gross profit only** — ticketing and SaaS, net of payment and cloud cost. Sponsorship and civic revenue are excluded.

**Speaker notes.** The LTV construction is the most important sentence on the slide. *"If I allocated sponsorship and civic revenue across hosts, this would read 12:1. I took it out, because sponsorship is earned by aggregate reach, not by any individual host, and 12:1 would not survive your diligence."* Then name the soft spot before they find it: participant CAC of A$3.60 assumes community-led growth works. If acquisition inverts to paid, CAC moves toward A$18–25 and the marketing line triples.

## Anticipated hard questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **"Why hasn't Eventbrite done this?"** | Their fee structure cannot profitably serve a A$12 ticket, and their taxonomy cannot express cultural community. A 40-person Onam sadya is not a customer they want. It is our entire market. |
| **"Isn't this just a listings site?"** | Listings do not have verified attendance data. That is what makes the civic product sellable and the acquittal report valuable, and it is why check-in integrity is our most important control. |
| **"Cultural discovery is local — how do you defend Sydney?"** | We do not claim network effects protect us. We name that risk. The defence is speed to the community graph and civic contract in each market, which is why we require an anchor partner before entering one. |
| **"Grant income looks like a crutch."** | The business is EBITDA-negative with or without grants and this round is sized without them. Grants buy CAC in the hardest-to-reach supply. |
| **"You're one person."** | Correct, and it is the highest-likelihood risk in our register. First hire is a senior engineer in month 1. Architecture is documented, infrastructure is fully in CDK. |
| **"A$58M SAM is small for venture."** | Australia is the proving ground, not the market. The same structure exists in ~120 metros; the global extension is A$1.0–2.5B. We would rather show you a defensible A$58M than an indefensible trillion. |
| **"What kills this company?"** | A community trust breach. Not competition. If we are ever seen as extracting from the communities we serve, the network that carries us also destroys us — which is why free stays free, individual data is never sold, and the Advisory Council is paid and binding. |
