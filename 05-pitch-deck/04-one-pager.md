# CulturePass — One Pager

**The cultural participation layer for multicultural cities**
Bibin Jose, Founder & CEO · cultureos.co@gmail.com · Melbourne, Australia

---

**THE PROBLEM.** Cultural life in multicultural cities is large, real, and invisible to infrastructure. Discovery runs on WhatsApp lists and closed Facebook groups, so you have to already be inside a community to find its events. Organisers face ticketing fees calibrated for A$80 tickets that consume 15–20% of a A$12 one, so they take cash at the door and keep no data. And councils and multicultural bodies distribute significant public money for cultural programming, then receive back attendance estimates written on the night. There is no instrument.

**THE SOLUTION.** A mobile-first platform — built, working, 39 screens on production AWS infrastructure — that does four things: **discovery** (city feed, natural-language search in community languages, map, cultural calendar, organised by community rather than generic category), **participation** (free RSVP, low-fee ticketing, digital passes with signed QR codes, gate check-in that rejects duplicate, cancelled and wrong-event scans), **organiser tooling** (event wizard, guest lists, verified badges, and grant acquittal reports), and **measurement** (first-party analytics producing the participation, representation and dispersal data nobody currently supplies).

**BUSINESS MODEL.** Ticketing at 3.5% + A$0.80 per paid ticket — free RSVP events are free forever, architecturally. Organiser SaaS at A$29/59/99 per month. Category-exclusive brand sponsorship. Civic participation-dashboard licences at A$35–75k per year. Culture Passion Awards program sponsorship. Five lines, sequenced so none is load-bearing.

**MARKET.** ~50,000 organisations across five Australian metros run at least one public cultural event a year. At A$1,164 of platform revenue per active host, that is a **~A$58M Australian SAM** — and the FY29 plan captures under 10% of it. The same structure exists in roughly 120 comparable metros worldwide, implying a A$1.0–2.5B global extension.

**UNIT ECONOMICS.** Host CAC A$180 · 3-year host LTV A$728 · **LTV:CAC 4.0:1** · participant CAC A$3.60 blended. LTV is built from host-attributable gross profit only — ticketing and SaaS, net of payment and cloud cost. Sponsorship and civic revenue are deliberately excluded; including them would show 12:1 and would not survive diligence.

**MOAT.** Not the code — that is replicable in six months and we do not claim otherwise. The moat is the community graph (verified hosts, follow relationships, cultural taxonomy and language mappings, built through relational fieldwork with consent — unscrapable), civic embedment (once a council reports statutory participation from our data, leaving means re-baselining a time series), grant acquittal lock-in, and the Culture Passion Awards as an owned institution.

**THE ASK.** **A$2.5M seed** — 45% product & engineering, 35% growth & community, 20% operations & compliance. Funds FY27 in full plus H1 FY28. Alongside it, a A$2.55M non-dilutive grant target across FY27–FY29 that subsidises market-entry CAC in the communities commercial CAC cannot reach. A **Series A of ~A$4.5M in FY28 is explicitly gated** on ten FY27 exit criteria; if they are missed, the plan contracts to Melbourne-only at 9 staff and runs to grant-plus-sponsorship breakeven rather than raising into weak retention.

**TEAM.** Bibin Jose, Founder & CEO — built the platform end to end: React frontend, Express API, PostgreSQL data model, AWS CDK infrastructure. First hire is a senior engineer in month one, to remove the single-engineer dependency that is currently the highest-likelihood risk in the register.

**WHAT IS NOT YET TRUE.** No paying customers. No signed sponsors. No committed grants. No launched pilot. Every figure above is a planning assumption traced to an explicit driver in a model available in full, including its downside cases. The drivers are the thing to challenge.
